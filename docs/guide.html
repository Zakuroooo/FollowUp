01The idea in one minute

Denise fixes fridges and freezers for restaurants and shops. Customers reach her in five ways: phone calls, texts, her website form, emails and referrals. She used to keep it all in her head and a notebook, and one forgotten callback lost her a $2,000 job.

FollowUp puts every request in one list, tells her who to call today and why, and alerts her the moment something urgent arrives, and again every 3 minutes until she acts.

Simple words mein: chahe customer call kare, text kare, email kare ya website form bhare, sab ek jagah aata hai, aur app khud yaad dilata hai kisko call karna hai.

  5. End of the week.The Numbers page answers her husband: open jobs, money waiting, won and lost, and why.

05Alerts: how she never misses one

sequenceDiagram
  participant C as Customer
  participant F as FollowUp
  participant D as Denise
  C->>F: "Freezer is down!" (any door)
  F->>D: Phone alert + sound + email (EMERGENCY)
  Note over D: Busy on another job...
  F->>D: 3 min later: "Still waiting: EMERGENCY Russo's"
  F->>D: 6 min later: again (up to 10 times)
  D->>F: Calls them / taps "I'm on it"
  Note over F: Alerts stop
On her phone

A notification even when FollowUp is closed. Emergencies stay on screen until tapped. On iPhone: add FollowUp to the Home Screen first.

On the computer

A chime for new requests, three sharp beeps for emergencies, the tab title flashes, and a red bar sits on top of every page.

Every 3 minutes

An emergency nobody acted on alerts again, up to 10 times (30 minutes). Stops when she calls, moves the job, or taps "I'm on it".

Email

An instant email for each new request. It shows the customer's name, and Reply goes to the customer. Plus the 7 AM list.

Emergency ke liye "baar baar yaad dilana" — har 3 minute, jab tak woh action na le. 10 baar ke baad ruk jaata hai taaki pareshaan na kare.

06Technicians and scheduling

Denise said tech scheduling would be "nice later", so it's kept simple:

  1. Add techs once.Settings → Technicians: "Carlos, Mike, Jen".
  2. Book a visit.When a customer says yes, she picks the date and the tech on the job page.
  3. See who's where.The Schedule page shows the next 14 days by day, filterable per tech, plus "said yes, needs a date" and "visit date passed".

09What's new (and where the ideas came from)

Ideas were taken from Denise's call first; other submissions (Callboard, KeepWarm) only helped spot gaps. Nothing was copied: every feature here was designed and written for FollowUp.

Five doors, one pipelineCalls, voicemails, texts, emails, website and referrals all go through the same steps: read → match customer → save words → alert.

SecurityEach business only sees its own data (enforced by the database). Calls/texts are signature-checked; website/email can't change urgency of existing jobs. 9 issues found by automatic review, all fixed and tested.

11Your to-do, in order

  1. Fix the keys (2 minutes).In your terminal: cd ~/Desktop/followup && bash scripts/set-keys.sh. It copies the missing Supabase key by itself and asks for Groq and Resend (hidden typing).
  2. Resend key.resend.com → sign up with your email → API Keys → Create API Key (permission: Sending access) → copy it and paste it into the script's prompt. Free plan: emails go to the address you signed up with until you verify a domain, which is fine for the demo.
  3. Tell Claude "keys done".It redeploys and tests the live site end to end.
  4. Record a Loom.Loom (loom.com) is a free screen recorder: you record your screen and voice, and it gives you a link to share. Install the Chrome extension, click Record → "Screen + Camera", follow the 2-minute script in GUSHWORK-LOOM-AND-QA.md, stop, copy the link.
  5. Submit the live link, the GitHub link and the Loom link on the Newton sheet before 7 PM.
